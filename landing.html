What you get

One small box that watches the weather and the noise around it, and tells you the moment something changes.

Live readings

Sound level and rain wetness update every second, straight from the sensors.

Charts over time

See the last minute of readings and exactly where they cross your limits.

Buzzer alerts

The buzzer sounds when wetness reaches 30% or noise reaches 70%. Mute it from the dashboard at any time.

Fully offline

The Pico sends its data over a USB cable. No Wi-Fi, hotspot or internet connection is needed.

How it works

Three steps from a raindrop to your screen.

  1. 1

    The sensors measure

    The sound sensor and the raindrop sensor send their analog signals to the Raspberry Pi Pico.

  2. 2

    The Pico reports over USB

    Twice a second it sends both readings to your PC, and switches the buzzer on or off when the PC tells it to.

  3. 3

    The dashboard shows it

    A small Flask program on your PC reads the USB port and serves this website at localhost:5000.

Hardware and wiring

Everything connects to the Pico. No extra chips are needed.

PartPico pinNotes
Sound sensor (KY-038 or LM393) GP26 Analog output (AO) to GP26. Power from 3V3(OUT).
Raindrop sensor (YL-83 or FC-37) GP27 Analog output (AO) to GP27. Power from 3V3(OUT).
Active buzzer GP15 Positive leg (+) to GP15, negative leg (−) to GND.
Raspberry Pi Pico or Pico W USB Plugs into the PC for power and data. Wi-Fi is not used.

Power both sensors from 3V3(OUT), not 5V. The Pico's inputs can only take 3.3V.
